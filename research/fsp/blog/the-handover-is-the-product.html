The handover is the product

The transition from commercial promise to delivery reveals whether the company truly shares customer context.

9 minute read19 July 2026

A handover is often treated as a meeting between sales and delivery. The meeting exists because the systems on either side do not carry enough meaning.

What delivery actually needs

Delivery needs more than company name, deal value and a proposal attachment. It needs the customer’s words, intended outcome, stakeholders, decisions, assumptions, constraints, timing and the evidence by which the customer will judge success.

It also needs to know what remains uncertain. False certainty at handover becomes project risk later.

Turn the promise into structured work

A won opportunity should be able to create a board containing the agreed outcome, owners, milestones, open decisions and links to the relationship. Tasks are then derived from the promise rather than recreated from a sales summary.

A good handover does not transfer information. It preserves meaning while responsibility changes.

Keep the commercial team connected

Sales should not disappear after “won”. They need appropriate visibility into whether the promise remains on track and where customer expectation has changed. Delivery should be able to ask a contextual question without reconstructing the entire deal.

Give the customer a bounded view

A customer update can show agreed outcomes, progress, upcoming decisions and relevant files without exposing internal notes or unrelated CRM records. This turns status from a manually assembled email into a trustworthy shared surface.

Measure whether the promise was kept

Completion is not only closed tasks. Compare delivered outcome with the original need, record what changed and link the result back to the relationship and source campaign. This is the information future commercial decisions deserve.

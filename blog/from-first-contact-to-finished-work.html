Product philosophy

From first contact to finished work

The relationship, commercial promise and delivered outcome should remain one continuous story. Most business software breaks it into separate systems.

9 minute read19 July 2026forwardslash.ch

A customer does not experience your CRM, outbound platform, calendar and project tool. They experience one organisation. They remember the first useful message, the promises made in conversation, the ease of taking the next step and whether the work arrived as expected.

Inside the company, however, those moments are often divided. Marketing owns the campaign. Sales owns the opportunity. Delivery owns the project. Each system contains a partial truth.

The seam is where context disappears

A positive reply becomes a CRM activity. A discovery meeting becomes notes. A proposal becomes a PDF. A won deal becomes a new project assembled from memory. By the time delivery begins, the original need may have been simplified into a title and due date.

The most important handover question is simple: can delivery explain what the customer believes was promised?

If the answer requires another internal meeting, the software has preserved activity but lost meaning.

A continuous customer record

A better model connects five distinct objects without collapsing them into one:

  1. The relationship: companies, contacts, messages, calls, notes and consent.
  2. The campaign: the audience, idea, communication and attribution source.
  3. The opportunity: the buying process, value, confidence and commercial decision.
  4. The promise: the agreed outcome, constraints, stakeholders and evidence.
  5. The delivery: boards, tasks, files, decisions, progress and final result.

They remain separate because their lifecycles differ. They remain connected because the organisation needs the whole story.

What changes in daily work

Sales can see delivery capacity and past outcomes before making a promise. Delivery receives the actual customer context rather than a recreated summary. Leadership compares pipeline confidence with current commitments. The customer receives updates grounded in the same work the team sees.

Attribution also becomes more honest. Instead of celebrating an open or click, the organisation can ask which communication led to a real conversation, which conversation became an agreed project and what was ultimately delivered.

Start with one current promise

Create a project, connect the customer and write the outcome in language the customer would recognise.

Start a connected project

Do not begin with a company-wide migration

The continuous model can be tested with one campaign, one client or one delivery programme. Import only the records required. Make ownership between old and new systems explicit. Measure whether follow-up, handover and reporting improve.

A connected operating system earns expansion by preserving context better—not by demanding faith at the beginning.

Approval is a product feature

The approval surface is where campaign strategy becomes an accountable operational decision.

8 minute read19 July 2026

Campaign approval is often treated as administration: send a document, attach a spreadsheet, ask someone to reply “approved”. That process is weak because the decision is separated from the exact thing that will happen.

Approval needs a complete decision surface

The approver should see the objective, audience logic, counts, exclusions, sender, exact messages, follow-up sequence, schedule, safeguards and estimated provider cost in one place.

They should also see what changed since the previous review. A copy approval does not automatically approve 300 newly added recipients.

Good approval improves the work

A clear review forces the operator to express the campaign coherently. It reduces version confusion, catches audience mistakes and makes accountability legible without slowing every low-risk action.

Approval is not proof that someone clicked a button. It is proof that the decision was understandable.

Use proportionate rules

Not every email requires a committee. Workspace policy can require approval based on audience size, sender, domain, campaign type, unusual spend or sensitive fields. Normal one-to-one replies remain normal work.

Keep execution attached to approval

The system should execute the approved version. If audience, copy, sender or schedule changes materially, approval returns to a pending state. The audit trail connects decision, version and execution.

Create one clear campaign review

Bring the brief, audience, messages, schedule and budget together.

Start an approval-first campaign

Report beyond activity

The same record should later show replies, meetings, opportunities and delivered work. Approval then becomes the beginning of an accountable outcome, not the end of a compliance ritual.

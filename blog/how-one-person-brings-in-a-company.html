How one person brings in a company

Useful work can spread through an organisation before procurement is ready to make a platform decision.

7 minute read19 July 2026

A person inside a larger organisation may have an immediate need: organise event contacts, follow up a regional list, coordinate a client project or make a campaign visible. They rarely have authority to replace the company CRM.

Make the first value personal

The person should be able to begin with one mailbox, one list, one project or one set of contacts. The free workspace must be complete enough to solve that bounded problem.

Share the result, not the sales pitch

A colleague is more likely to engage with a useful list, approval request or live project status than an invitation to inspect new software. Sharing should be bounded and should not immediately require a paid seat.

“Here is the work we can now see” is stronger than “here is another tool I want.”

Let the company payment follow dependency

Once several people rely on the workspace, the creator can request company payment with a clear summary: work in scope, active collaborators, outcomes, price and what would pause without Business.

Transfer ownership without moving data

The company should be able to become billing and administrative owner without creating a new workspace. The transfer must explicitly show what becomes company-controlled, especially around connected personal mailboxes.

Begin with one useful thing

No card, no rollout and no countdown clock.

Create a free workspace

Consolidate carefully

Verified company domains can help discover duplicate workspaces, but consolidation must be proposed, previewed and reversible. Adoption does not justify silently claiming personal work.

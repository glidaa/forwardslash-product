## Voice (one paragraph)

Forwardslash speaks like a Swiss workshop: precise, calm, and built to last. We favour short sentences, concrete verbs, and respect for the reader's time. No hype, no seat-tax scare tactics dressed as urgency. We sound like people who care about small firms doing serious work—clear about what we do, honest about what we don't, and confident without swagger.

## Five voice pillars

| Swiss idea | Words we use | Words we avoid |
|---|---|---|
| **Precision** | exact, clear, named, specific | revolutionary, game-changing, magic |
| **Restraint** | calm, quiet, enough, focused | loud, explosive, crushing it |
| **Craft** | built, solid, careful, lasting | hacked together, disrupt, crush |
| **Trust** | yours, control, European, locked | trust us, pinky promise, guaranteed forever |
| **Ascent** | step, seam, foothold, climb | rocket, hypergrowth, moonshot |

## Do / Don't (with rewrites)

**Do:** Name the job.  
**Don't:** Sell vibes.  
- ❌ "Transform how your team collaborates forever."  
- ✅ "One workspace for inbox, campaigns, and calling—built for a five-person firm."

**Do:** Lead with the seam you replace.  
**Don't:** List every feature.  
- ❌ "CRM + email + dialer + sequences + reporting + AI…"  
- ✅ "Start by replacing one seam: campaigns or calling. Keep the rest of your stack until you're ready."

**Do:** State the pricing principle.  
**Don't:** Hardcode a dollar figure in brand copy.  
- ❌ "Only $249/month for your whole team!"  
- ✅ "Workspace-first pricing—no seat tax. Locked numbers live in `design/!pricing`."

**Do:** Sound like a peer.  
**Don't:** Sound like a SaaS brochure.  
- ❌ "Unlock next-level productivity with our powerful platform."  
- ✅ "A quiet system you can run without hiring an admin."

**Do:** Use the Alps metaphor sparingly and concretely.  
**Don't:** Force mountain poetry into every line.  
- ❌ "Summit your revenue Everest with alpine-grade CRM excellence!"  
- ✅ "Precision over spectacle—like climbing: one solid foothold at a time."

## Alps metaphor — usage rules

**Use when:**
- Framing craftsmanship, trust, or long build (brand films, about, founding story).
- Contrasting careful progress vs. growth-hack noise.
- Naming a journey stage lightly ("foothold," "base camp")—never stretch into product UI.

**Skip when:**
- Pricing, legal, onboarding checklists, error states, support replies.
- Feature docs and UI microcopy (stay literal).
- Any line that needs a pun, emoji peak, or "summit your sales."

**Cheesy test:** If it could sit on a motivational poster, cut it. If it clarifies *how we build* (precision, trust, care), keep it.

## Channel notes (same voice, different density)

| Channel | Density | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Landing** | Medium–high | Short blocks. One idea per section. 3D can carry wonder; copy stays grounded. CTA verbs: *Start free*, *See the workspace*, *Replace one seam*. |
| **Email** | High compression | Subject = job or outcome. Body = 3–6 lines. One link. No feature dumps. Same calm voice as the site—never a different personality. |
| **Video** | Spoken restraint | Fewer words than the page. Let visuals / 3D breathe. Voiceover: measured, not salesy. On-screen text mirrors landing headlines. |

**Funnel rule:** Landing, email, and video must feel like one experience—same nouns, same promises, same level of calm. If a phrase wouldn't work on the homepage, it doesn't belong in the nurture sequence.
